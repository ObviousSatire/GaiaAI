What GAIA is

A system that reads the genome and reads the web. It discovers patterns, grows a knowledge graph, and improves continuously as new data arrives — from files, from databases, and from hundreds of curated reputable sources.

Most drug discovery starts with a hypothesis and tests it. GAIA starts with the raw genome and the raw literature, and works backward. Nothing is hand-coded. The system discovers its own patterns.

How it learns

Three loops, running continuously. Each one improves the system without anyone telling it what to look for.

Loop 01

Reading raw sequence

Whole chromosomes go in. The system reads the structure hidden inside the sequence — the shape of information as it unfolds along the genome.

  • Ingest full chromosomes
  • Read shape, rhythm, and context
  • Discover what distinguishes one region from another
  • Validate across chromosomes and species
Loop 02

Growing the knowledge graph

Every new finding becomes a new edge. Variants link to genes. Genes link to pathways. Pathways link to diseases. Diseases link to compounds. The graph grows with every paper published.

  • Ingest mappings, bindings, structures
  • Connect variant → gene → disease → compound
  • Query score candidates, rank outcomes
  • Retrain new pairs improve predictions
Loop 03

The continuous cycle

Every prediction produces new data, and new data updates the model. If a new feature helps, the classifier keeps it. If it doesn't, the weight fades. Nothing is set in stone.

  • New evidence variant, paper, structure
  • Context fetched class, conservation, motif
  • Model retrains weights adjust on new inputs
  • Impact measured help stays, noise fades

The math underneath

Sequence is raw potential. Meaning emerges only when a pattern is applied in a context. We wrote the math that reads both — not assembled from other people's tools, but derived from the structure of the genome itself.

Boundaries are where biology shifts

The genome isn't uniform. Composition drifts, structure loosens, conservation fades. A boundary is placed wherever those shifts align — and the same logic runs at three scales at once.

Every segment earns a signature

Between boundaries, each region is described not by a label but by a fingerprint — its composition, its reading frame, its skew, its conservation across deep time. The classifier is not told what to look for. It discovers which combinations of features separate one kind of region from another, and holds that separation across chromosomes and species.

No thresholds are hand-set. No rules are hard-coded. The boundaries, the features, and the decision surface are all discovered from labeled sequence — and every equation is written from scratch.

Evidence across every layer

Predictions are checked against three independent kinds of evidence — computational, cellular, physiological. A claim that survives all three is worth acting on.

In silico

Computational

Every prediction is stress-tested in pure computation before anything touches a cell. Confidence is estimated from the spread of agreement across independent methods.

  • Structure prediction — modeled protein geometry
  • Binding simulation — strength across methods
  • Cross-validation — held-out data
  • Confidence intervals — agreement across approaches
In vitro

Cellular

Predictions are compared against real cell-based assays from public literature. Pathway activity, gene expression, and cytotoxicity all feed back into the model.

  • Cell-line assays — published screens
  • Pathway response — signaling under treatment
  • Gene expression — transcript-level confirmation
  • Cytotoxicity profiles — safe dose ranges
In vivo

Physiological

Protocols are evaluated against animal model studies and human trial data. Where the model predicts an outcome, that outcome is checked against published physiological responses.

  • Animal disease models — established systems
  • Clinical trial data — dosing, outcomes, events
  • Human cohorts — time-to-response
  • Longitudinal outcomes — durability at follow-up

Cross-cutting validation

Cross-species
Patterns that hold across evolutionary distance are more likely to reflect real biology.
Animal models
Predictions checked against real physiological responses in established model systems.
Human trial models
Protocols evaluated against clinical data — dosing, outcomes, adverse events, timing.
Held-out evaluation
Tested on chromosomes, diseases, and compounds never seen during training.

What makes GAIA different

Most tools solve one piece.
GAIA runs the whole chain — from raw sequence to treatment protocol — inside a single self-learning system.

i.

End-to-end, not piecemeal

Genome scanning, variant mapping, compound screening, and protocol design usually live in separate systems — stitched together by hand, translated between file formats, re-explained at every seam. GAIA runs the whole chain as one pipeline. A signal found at one end is already legible at the other. Nothing has to be handed off. Nothing is lost in translation.

ii.

Learned, not configured

Weights are discovered from labeled sequence. Features are extracted, tested, and kept only if they carry signal. There are no fixed scoring functions, no hand-tuned rules, no manual configuration for every new target. As the knowledge graph grows, the model sharpens — without being rebuilt from scratch each time a new disease enters the frame.

iii.

Derived, not assembled

Boundary detection across three scales. A feature space built from first principles. A classifier that discovers its own thresholds from labeled sequence. Nothing here is a wrapper around someone else's tool. Every equation is written from scratch — because the genome doesn't care what library you imported. It only cares whether the math is right.

iv.

Checked before it speaks

Every prediction is verified against three independent layers — computational, cellular, physiological. A claim that survives all three is worth acting on. A claim that survives only computation is not a claim. It is a guess — and it does not get a protocol. Validation here is not a downstream step run by a separate team. It is built into the claim itself.
